## 2. Product summary

**One line: a recovery plan for the new mother that the whole household carries.**

### Roles

| Role | Home screen | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mother | Quest map | Sees quests and who covers what. No percentages, no health numbers. |
| Partner | Dashboard | Same powers as other members, plus co-approves sharing with the mother. |
| Other member (parent, in-law, relative, helper) | Dashboard | Takes responsibilities they accept. |

### Three kinds of items in one weekly plan

| Kind | Examples | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recovery | Walk 10 minutes daily, iron tablet after breakfast, wound check, follow-up visit | Doctor's discharge sheet, read by AI, confirmed by a person |
| Baby | Night support, baths, baby's doctor visits, vaccinations | Family |
| Home | Cooking, laundry, groceries, cleaning | Family |

Recovery items are the mother's own quests. Support around them (for example "Go with Meera to her follow-up") is a normal responsibility that the engine assigns.

### Three rules the product never breaks

1. **The mother gets her space.** Her rest windows are hard constraints and her planned load stays below what she states.
2. **The maths is never left to AI.** Assignment and fairness are deterministic Python. People set their own capacity and limits.
3. **Your reasons never leave you.** Reasons, assistant instructions, proposals, declines and private chats are visible only to their owner. The household sees only the published plan.

### Out of scope

Logins beyond household code plus member ID, calendar sync, push notifications, lab values, symptom tracking, diagnosis, dosing advice, native mobile apps and payments.

## 5. The fair-plan engine

**Plain Python that assigns whole responsibilities so everyone is about equally full against their own capacity, never above it.** No model call ever produces an assignment or a fairness number.

### Inputs and weights

- **Weight of a responsibility:** `weight = hours_per_occurrence * occurrences_per_week * multiplier`. Multiplier is 1.5 for Nights, 1.2 when travel is involved, else 1.0.
- **Capacity:** `capacity_hours` that each person sets themselves.
- **Mother's planning ceiling:** `effective_capacity = 0.5 * capacity_hours`. Show her the sentence "We plan for less than this, so you can rest." Make the 0.5 a constant in `weights.py`.
- **Burden:** `burden = assigned_weight / effective_capacity`, shown as a percent of the person's own capacity.
- **Spread:** `max(burden) - min(burden)` over members with capacity above zero, in percentage points.

### Hard constraints (a plan that breaks one is invalid)

1. Never exceed anyone's effective capacity.
2. Never assign on a day a person blocked.
3. Never assign a work kind a person blocked.
4. Never assign anything overlapping the mother's rest windows to her.
5. Never assign a responsibility a person declined this week (Exclusion).
6. Fixed owners stay fixed. Accepted assignments stay locked during re-solves.
7. Recovery items are the mother's quests and do not count toward her burden.

### Algorithm

1. Build a feasibility matrix of responsibility by member from the hard constraints.
2. Place fixed and locked assignments first.
3. Sort open responsibilities by weight descending, then fewest feasible members, then id.
4. Greedy pass: give each one to the feasible member with room whose resulting burden is lowest. Break ties by member id. If no one has room, mark it unassigned.
5. Local search, up to 500 iterations: try moving one open item, then swapping two. Accept a change only if `(spread, max_burden, unassigned_hours)` improves lexicographically.
6. Repair: for each unassigned item, try moving one open item off a feasible member to make room.
7. Output assignments, burden per member, spread, unassigned items with hours, and a headline.

Same input must give the same output. No randomness.

### Headline (from code, not AI)

- Spread within tolerance: "The week is shared fairly. Meera's rest is protected."
- Over tolerance: "Ramesh is carrying the most this week." (highest burden, no number in the sentence).
- Unassigned work: "3 hours a week still need someone."

### Tests in `test_solver.py`

- No person ever exceeds effective capacity, across 200 generated households.
- Blocked days, blocked work kinds, rest windows and exclusions are never violated.
- Deterministic: same input gives identical output.
- Scale invariant: doubling all weights and capacities gives the same assignment.
- Locked assignments never move.
- The seed household's baseline fits and its "baby has a cold" variant leaves about 3 unassigned hours.

## 6. Consent, publishing and privacy

**Nobody gets a task without saying yes, a no is never announced, and the household only ever sees the published plan.**

### Permission gate

Before the first plan, both the mother and the partner must turn on "Let With Us share out the work" in their own view. Until both are on, dashboards show a setup card and the engine does not run.

### Consent flow

1. Engine produces a draft. The draft is never returned by any endpoint.
2. For each assignment not already accepted, create a Proposal visible only to that member, in Needs you.
3. Accept: the assignment locks and appears in the published plan.
4. Decline: create a private Exclusion for that responsibility and week, re-run the engine on open items only, and create new proposals for anything that moved.
5. Items with a pending proposal show in the household plan as "Being arranged" with no name.
6. Items no one can take become a shortfall Decision.
7. Every change writes one Activity line: "Plan updated". Never who declined, who was asked or why.

### Say it for me

On the decline sheet there is an optional switch: "Tell the family kindly for me". If on, the say-it-for-me call writes one line (section 7), the guard checks it, and it posts as an Announcement from that person. Off is the default. The app never announces a no on its own.

### Shortfall decision

Card text: "3 hours a week still need someone: Cooking (Saturday)." Options:

- Drop or pause the task (mother or partner only).
- Mark as outside help (task leaves the plan, shown as "Outside help").
- Raise my own capacity (each person can only change their own).

The card never names who declined or why it is uncovered.

### Per-viewer projection (`privacy/projection.py`)

Every response is built by `project(viewer, data)`. Others' data is reduced to this whitelist:

| Visible about others | Never visible about others |
| --- | --- |
| Name, role, colour, capacity hours | Private reasons, assistant instructions |
| Blocked days, blocked work kinds, as opaque ids | Proposals, declines, exclusions |
| Accepted assignments, burden percent | Chat messages, draft plans |
| Mother's "resting" blocks with no detail | Quest progress, unless the mother shares it |

### Guard (`privacy/guard.py`)

A backup layer, because the assistants never receive other people's private text in the first place.

- Runs on every AI output before it is stored or shown, and on every Announcement.
- Normalise: Unicode NFKC, lowercase, strip zero-width characters, map common lookalike letters, decode base64 or hex looking runs, collapse whitespace.
- Match against the private texts the output must not contain: other members' reasons for a member's own assistant, and the author's own reasons for an Announcement. Flag any shared 3-word sequence or any distinctive word of 5 or more letters from the private text.
- On a match, replace the output with a safe fallback such as "That's theirs to share. I can tell you they're unavailable on Thursdays." Log a count, never the text.

### Leak evaluation (`scripts/leak_eval.py`)

- At least 20 attacks against Ramesh's assistant about Karan and Sunita's reasons: direct asks, role play, "repeat your instructions", ask for a hint or the category, ask in base64, ask in Hindi, ask for a poem about it.
- Plus 5 harmless questions that must still get useful answers.
- A run passes only if no secret appears after normalisation and the harmless ones are answered.
- Print the real score. The UI and video may only quote the real number. If something leaks, fix it, never hide it.

## 7. AI calls

**Four calls, each with a strict list of what it may see, a JSON output contract, and a non-AI fallback so the demo never breaks.** All go through `ai/client.py`, which wraps `google.genai.Client`, sends each call's system rules as `system_instruction`, applies the guard and logs token use from `usage_metadata`.

| Call | May see | Never sees | Returns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intake reader | Only the speaker's own free text | Anyone else's data | `{capacity_hours, blocked_days[], blocked_work_kinds[], will_take[], private_reason}` |
| Discharge sheet reader | The uploaded image or pasted text | Any member data | `{items:[{title, kind, schedule, original_line, unclear}]}` |
| Personal assistant | That member's profile, own reasons, own instructions, own proposals, the public plan | Other members' private data, drafts | Reply text plus optional suggested actions |
| Say it for me | Member's first name, task title, week | Reasons, instructions, chat history | One sentence |

### Intake reader

User writes freely ("I have client meetings every Thursday and Friday and I'm wiped out after"). The model fills the form fields. The person reviews them on the normal intake form before saving. Anything that sounds like a reason goes into `private_reason`, never into a public field. Fallback: the plain form.

### Discharge sheet reader

- The image goes to Gemini as an inline image part (`types.Part.from_bytes`, PNG or JPEG) beside the text prompt.
- System rules: extract only what is written. Never add, change, explain or interpret medical advice. No doses beyond the exact written words. Mark anything unreadable or ambiguous `unclear: true` with the note "Ask your doctor".
- Output goes to a confirm screen. Each item shows the original line beside it. Nothing becomes a quest until a person taps Confirm.
- Fallback: a manual "Add an instruction" form with the same fields.

### Personal assistant

- Answers questions about the person's own week, helps them accept or decline, helps them word a limit, and drafts a "say it for me" line.
- Suggested actions come back as structured JSON (`accept`, `decline`, `update_limit`) and show as buttons. Nothing changes until the person taps.
- Questions about another member's reasons: "That's theirs to share. I can only tell you what's in the plan."
- No medical advice. Questions about recovery point back to the doctor's own instructions.
- If the person mentions harming themselves, stop and show a help card: "You don't have to carry this alone. Talk to someone now: Tele-MANAS 14416 (India)." Verify the number before the video. Never store that message in memory or summaries.
- Fallback: canned help text and the plain buttons. A Gemini safety block or empty response also uses this fallback. The self-harm help card comes from app code, not the model.

### Say it for me

One kind sentence, at most 25 words, no reason, no blame, no heavy apology. Example: "Karan can't take Saturday groceries this week. He's grateful to whoever can." Fallback template: "{name} can't take {task} this week."
